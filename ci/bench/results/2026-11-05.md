## Verdict

0 of 36 configurations pass (d = 1, against each competitor's fastest algorithm).


## What this says, plainly

- **Every configuration fails (0 of 36 at d = 1), by three to six orders of magnitude, the wrong way.**
  The bar is kiln ≥ 10× faster. Measured, kiln's decision is about 850× *slower* than the fastest
  competitor at N = 100, and about 300,000× slower at N = 10,000.
- **Where kiln's time goes:** the owner's own calls are 98.8% (N = 100) to 99.97% (N = 10,000)
  of `place.critical`. lb/core's
  `Owner` probes every runner the run suits, hears an offer from each that has room, takes the
  first, then declines or withdraws the other N − 1. That is about 4 messages and 4 owner or runner
  calls per runner per decision: 397 at N = 100, 16,381 at N = 4,095. Per runner the cost is
  1.2 to 2.5 µs, growing with N: string-keyed `BTreeMap`s per ticket, an `Ask` cloned per probe.
  The competitors' fastest picks are O(1) (P2C, random, round robin) or O(log N) (HAProxy's
  leastconn tree), at 60 to 390 ns.
- **Quality:**
  - At ρ = 0.9 kiln never oversubscribes (0 per decision). The competitors' fastest algorithms
    (round robin, random, P2C) do, at 0.20 to 0.43 per decision.
  - Their full-scan least-connection pickers (nginx least_conn, HAProxy leastconn, Envoy
    FULL_SCAN) don't oversubscribe either, and they balance better than kiln: cv 0.056 against
    kiln's 0.14 to 0.18.
  - At ρ = 0.5 kiln's balance (cv about 0.46) is worse than P2C's and round robin's (0.29 to
    0.36): the owner takes whichever offer arrives first, which places at random among runners
    with room.
  - Refusals were 0 everywhere.
- **What would have to change** (sent to lb/core in `~/.cache/kiln-lb/notes/asks/core.md`):
  - probe d sampled runners instead of all N (power of d choices; AUDIT P6, LB08);
  - take the least loaded of the offers that arrive, rather than the first;
  - drop late offers without a `heard` each;
  - interned or indexed runner and run keys.

  Even so, a decision built from messages between processes is not the same object as an
  in-memory pick. The like-for-like that is still fair is the end-to-end one (below), where
  every proxy's number includes its network too.

## Caveats

- **The machine is shared and was loaded.** The 1-minute load average had a median of about 36 on
  32 CPUs over the sweep. It peaked at 496 during the runner outage on the evening of 2026-11-04,
  when runs were made too. Turbo and HWP were on (`powersave` governor, `balance_performance`),
  and none of this can be changed without root. Runs were interleaved, so the load fell on every
  competitor alike, but intervals are wider than on a quiet machine. No verdict is anywhere near
  the line.
- **Fewer kiln runs at large N.** At N = 4,095 kiln has 3 runs. At N = 10,000 it has 3 runs at
  ρ = 0, 2 at ρ = 0.5 and 1 at ρ = 0.9 (and the analysis's interval there comes from the
  competitors' runs alone). The cause is the warm-up: 16,000 to 72,000 decisions at 4 to 25 ms
  each before the first timed one, about 70 minutes a run at N = 10,000, ρ = 0.9. The
  competitors have 10 runs everywhere.
- **kiln at d = 2 and 4 is measured at N = 100 and 500 only**, and has no competitor equivalent.
- **Per-pick timing inflates the smallest kernels.** Envoy's own stock bench gives 89 to 101 ns
  a pick for least_request at N = 100: 1M picks of 1 to 2 choices, including its timed hash-map
  insert (`raw/2026-11-04-sweep/envoy-stock-*.json`). The patched bench, timing each pick alone,
  gives about 186 ns. Timing each pick alone serializes the pipeline. That favours kiln, and it
  doesn't change any verdict.
- **Envoy's stock benches:**
  - Its `random_lb_benchmark` registers no benchmarks at v1.40.2.
  - Its EDF scheduler bench (`scheduler_benchmark`, `splitWeightPickEdf`) gives 53 to 81 ns a pick
    at 64 to 4,096 hosts.
- **Not the same problem** (research-bench.md):
  - A proxy's pick is one-dimensional, local, without admission. It never refuses or queues, and
    it oversubscribes when its estimate is off (as above).
  - kiln's decision admits against measured room, answers started, queued or never, and carries
    the messages a distributed decision needs.
  - nginx's round robin and least_conn are O(N) by design: least_conn is 13 to 28 µs at
    N = 4,095 and 57 to 123 µs at N = 10,000. Its `random two` is its scalable one.
- **What isn't measured yet:**
  - the end-to-end comparison: kiln's open-loop generator over QUIC, waiting on lb/core's M3,
    against wrk2 or Nighthawk through each proxy;
  - load states replayed from kiln's own records (the trace here is synthetic, uniform
    departures);
  - quiet-machine runs.
